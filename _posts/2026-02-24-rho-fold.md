While looking into [part 2 of the Stanford RNA 3D folding challenge on kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/stanford-rna-3d-folding-2) we learned a lot about the structure of RNA and popular machine learning approaches for this task.

For a long time, the central dogma in biology was that the genetic information stored in our DNA (DeoxyriboNucleic Acid) is transcribed into RNA (RiboNucleic Acid), which in turn delivers the instructions to the cell structures (ribosomes) to build the corresponding proteins.
Today, we know that there are many different sorts of RNA floating around in our cells, and some of them are still not fully understood.
RNA molecules are basically long strings of nucleotides, where each nucleotide carries one of 4 nucleobases: Adenine (`A`), Guanine (`G`), Cytosine (`C`), or Uracil (`U`).
Depending on the base sequence, the RNA molecule folds in a way that usually minimizes its energy, and the resulting 3D structure determines the molecule's function.

In RNA structure prediction, your input is a base sequence (a string made up of the 4 letters `AGCU`), and you want your model to predict 3D coordinates for each base.
While models like AlphaFold exist to predict 3D structures of proteins (which can be also described as a sequence of building blocks, in this case amino acids), RNA structure prediction is still challenging due to much less data available.

One of the state of the art models for RNA structure prediction is `RhoFold+`:

- [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02487-0)
- [GitHub](https://github.com/ml4bio/RhoFold)
